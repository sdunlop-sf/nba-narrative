01The problem

The AI is fine. The system around it forgets.

A call, a claim, a renewal, a lapsed login: every signal is a moment in a relationship. Campaign tools answer some of them on a schedule. Central decision engines try to answer all of them, and over time they turn into a system the business stops using.

Brain at the centre

What usually happens

  • !Every change queues behind one team. New offer, copy tweak, channel rule: all through central specialists.
  • !The business stops going in. A separate tool, its own concepts and skills. Channel teams build work-arounds.
  • !A second copy of the customer. Data replicated into the engine brings latency, reconciliation and stale consent.
  • !No memory of what it decided. The decision is logged as an afterthought, so nothing compounds.

A decisioning harness

What we do instead

  • ✓A thin layer by design. Eligibility lives in segments, actions are catalogue records. There's no rules tree to maintain.
  • ✓It lives where people already work. Configured in the CRM the business already runs, with the same skills.
  • ✓One copy of the customer. It reads context where it lives, including the data warehouse, without copying it.
  • ✓Every decision is remembered. What, why, what was held back, and what happened. That record is the asset.

02The idea

The harness owns the moment. The channel owns the rest.

A decisioning harness is the governed layer every channel, app and agent calls to decide what happens next. In each lifecycle moment it decides what's right for this customer, and whether to act at all. The channel decides how it's delivered.

The harness owns

WhatWhich action or offer, or none, in this moment
WhetherConsent, suppression, fatigue, conflicts across teams
WhichWhich approved content, or delegated to the channel
StartFulfilment, through existing Salesforce Flows
RecordWhy it decided, and what happened

The channel owns

HowRendering, layout, creative production and testing
WhenExact send time, quiet hours, journey steps
MechanicsJourneys, placements and scripts
ReportingChannel engagement

One decision per customer

It arbitrates across every team's objectives (retention, cross-sell, service), so the customer gets the most relevant interaction, not the loudest campaign.

"Do nothing" is a decision

When the right answer is to hold back (an open complaint, hardship, recent contact), the harness records that choice and the reason.

The reasoning is pluggable

Rules, your own models, learning, and AI where it's justified. The reasoning can change; the governed record of decisions stays.

03Two sides of the work

Stop building campaigns. Stock a shelf, and let the harness decide from it.

Marketing splits into two sides. On the build side, people (working with AI) create and approve the actions, offers and content that go on a composable shelf. On the delivery side, the harness decides from that shelf for each customer, within guardrails the business sets.

Build side

Stock the shelf

Offers, content and short journeys are created, tagged and approved once, then reused everywhere. AI helps draft; people approve.

Delivery side

Decide from the shelf

The harness picks the right action, offer and approved content for each customer and moment, then the channel delivers it.

Governance

Set the guardrails

The business sets objectives, priorities and contact rules, and approves bigger changes the system proposes. Small adjustments apply automatically; larger ones need a person.

The four gaps it closes

Architecture

Journeys → moments

From fixed journeys and calendars to decisions made per customer, per moment, from a shelf.

Science

One model → layers

Scores, learning from outcomes, and AI reasoning for the hard calls, running together.

Operating model

Campaign teams → build & govern

Teams stock and curate the shelf and set guardrails, instead of hand-building every send.

Measurement

Clicks → lift

Holdouts and verified outcomes show what was incremental, not just what was clicked.

05Outbound meets inbound

One decision, two speeds

Lifecycle decisions are made ahead of time and kept current. Inbound channels serve that decision in the moment, and a new decision is made when context changes. An outcome in one channel updates all the others.

Decide ahead

Lifecycle & outbound

Decisions made on customer events and in daily cycles, across every objective and policy, then held as each customer's current next best action. Delivered through journeys and outbound teams.

Serve in the moment

Inbound

Web, app, contact centre and stores read the current decision. A call, a complaint or a decline triggers a fresh one.

Close the loop

Consistent everywhere

Accept in an email and the web tile disappears. Decline on a call and the next channel gets a different, better-suited action.

06Why memory wins

Every decision compounds. Every month you wait is learning you don't get back.

An AI model can make a decent decision on day one with no history. What it can't do on its own is remember what was decided, for whom, under which rules, and whether it worked. That memory is what makes month twelve better than month one.

Day 1

A good start

Scores from existing customer data. A capable starting point that any good model could match.

Month 1

It knows what works

Thousands of observed outcomes. Holdouts show what's genuinely incremental, and for whom.

Month 6

Patterns in sequences

Which action works after which, by segment. Fatigue and timing learned, not guessed.

Year 1

An advantage you can't copy

A competitor can rebuild the engine. They can't rebuild twelve months of decisions and outcomes.
