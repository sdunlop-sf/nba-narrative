Each era solved the constraint of the one before. Batch and blast couldn’t personalise, so we built journeys. Journeys couldn’t arbitrate across customer state, so we built decisioning engines. Decisioning engines can score within their training data, and the good ones adapt — but they can’t compose new strategies, hold the full relationship trajectory, or expose the optimisation objective for governance. That’s the constraint we’re removing now. The talk before this one walked you through modernising into the journey era. That work is the foundation. The fourth era is what gets built on top.

Channel memory is what your platforms know. Relationship memory is what your best human bankers know.
There are two kinds of memory. Channel memory is what your platforms know — open rates, variant winners, optimal send times. Everyone in this room has channel memory, and the modernisation work many of you are doing right now gets you a much better version of it. Relationship memory is what your best human bankers know. What we’ve offered, what they’ve said, what’s changed, what they need now. Almost nobody’s systems have that. The question is whether yours can.

Reasoning is pluggable.
The institutional learning is the moat.
When memory lives in the profile, an agent can do four things. Read the relationship state. Reason within the constraints you’ve defined. Act on the right channel. Write the outcome back to memory. Every decision makes the next one smarter. The reasoning engine in the middle is pluggable. Today it’s deterministic scoring with an LLM re-rank. In two years it’ll be something we haven’t named yet. That’s fine. That’s the point. Think of it like a flight recorder for customer relationships — every decision, every outcome, every constraint preserved and audited. Airlines aren’t safe because pilots keep getting smarter. They’re safe because the system around the pilot accumulates and learns. A competitor can copy the brain in a weekend. They cannot copy twelve months of compounding institutional learning.
